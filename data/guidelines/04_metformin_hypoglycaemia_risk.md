# Metformin, sulfonylureas and insulin when kidney function falls
Source: KDIGO 2022 Diabetes in CKD (Rec 4.1.1, Practice Points 4.1.x); US FDA metformin label; ADA Standards of Care 2026 sec 9. Status: verify.

- Metformin: continue at full dose when eGFR is 45 or more; at eGFR 30-44 do not start it and reduce the dose (about 1,000 mg per day maximum); stop below 30.
- Check vitamin B12 in long-term metformin users.
- Sulfonylureas and insulin can cause hypoglycaemia, and the risk rises as eGFR falls because the drugs are cleared more slowly. Prefer shorter-acting agents (for example gliclazide or glipizide) and lower doses in CKD.
- DPP-4 inhibitors are weight-neutral with low hypoglycaemia risk; most need dose adjustment for eGFR (linagliptin does not). Do not combine with a GLP-1 RA.
